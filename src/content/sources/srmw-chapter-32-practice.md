Practice! That’s what I was trying to do. That had become the point of my writing. Writing
to win NaNoWriMo was long in my past. I was writing my self-referential metanovel with its
imaginary God and imaginary Not Quite Omniscient Narrator and its imaginary reader to remind
me to do what I’d been reminding myself to do for years: to practice.
Practice would help me make changes in my life that I hadn’t been able to make. I had
wanted to live my life intentionally, but I had not, because I had not practiced.
“You are such a moron,” said my inner critic. “You’ve known that for years. You’ve re­
minded yourself that dozens of times. You’ve written more than 600 pages trying to remind
yourself of something that you’d know if you weren’t so stupid.”
“There’s a reason he’s doing this,” said God. “Shut up and let him think.”
“Yes,” said Mom. “Shut up!”
I thought. But I had no answer. I needed to remember to practice. But how could I remember
to practice unless I first practiced remembering. Which I never remembered to do.
“Can you give me a hint,” I asked God. “I believe that I have the answer, but I don’t know
how to articulate it.”
“Ask the doorknob,” God said. “The doorknob will tell you.”
