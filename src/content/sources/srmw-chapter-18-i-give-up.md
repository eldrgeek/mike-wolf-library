Narrative Chapter 1: An Accomplished Liar
I’d lied. Not to the muse’s face, but I’d lied in the metanovel, where I knew she’d find it.
I’d lied ten times! I’d done it!
By the end of Chapter 18, I had thought one lie, spoke three, and narrated the rest. Every
sentence in the chapter was a lie. Nearly every phrase was a lie.
I’d lied repeatedly, assuredly, and I hoped effectively.
And I felt really good! All that lying practice had paid off.
And now I was really ready to face the muse!
I waited, just long enough to give the muse time to read Chapter 18 while I moved a copy to
a backup metauniverse. Then I deleted the original.
I could hardly wait to lie to her face!
But when I looked for New Chapter 1, I found it was in the past, not the future.
I went back and read the chapter. The muse had been there, and she was pissed. And I found

There was a long pause. The writers looked at one another. My chest ached. I could feel
tears coming. I looked around the room hoping to see a friendly face. Only God’s face, serene,
did not register disgust or contempt. Finally Brenda Euland stood up.
“I taught writing for nearly fifty years,” she said. She looked at me. “You’ve read my
book If You Want to Write, haven’t you?”
“Yes,” I said, looking at the floor.
“Remember me saying: ‘Everyone is talented, original and has something important
to say?’” She asked.
“Yes, I do. It was a beautiful thought,” I said.
“Well,” she said, and she paused. She waited until I finally looked at her. “You’re
the exception. You’re not talented. You’re not original. And you have absolutely nothing
important to say.”
“That’s it!” God said. “I don’t like the way this chapter is going.” She waved her
hand and the chapter ended.
