<p>“<em>The World’s Stupidest Novel</em> is the world’s stupidest title for a novel, even if it is the title of the world’s stupidest novel,” says my inner critic.</p>

<p>My inner critic’s yammering voice has echoed in my head for as long as I can remember. It never misses a chance to point out the mistakes I’m making; to remind me of the mistakes I have made; to predict the mistakes that I will inevitably make; and to make sure I know that nothing that I do will ever, ever be good enough.</p>

<p>“ <em>The World’s Stupidest Novel</em> is not the title I see,” says a reader. Perhaps that reader was you, the person reading this sentence.</p>

<p>“That wasn’t me,” says another reader. “But I don’t see that title either.”</p>

<p>“Me either,” says yet another.</p>

<p>“That not the title anymore,” I explain. “I changed it while I was writing the first draft. That old title was just a placeholder.”</p>

<p><em>“Self-referential metanovel Writing for D*mmies: a self-referential metanovel?”</em> Asks my inner critic after I change the title. “At least I understood <em>The World’s Stupidest Novel.</em> It was appropriate. Your novel really was stupid. Still is. You need to change it again. No one will understand the new title.”</p>

<p>“The title stays,” I say. “I like it.”</p>

<p>“Well, I don’t like it,”says my inner critic. “And the whole book is stupid. Not just the title.”</p>

<p>“You never like anything, do you?” I ask my inner critic.</p>

<p>“I like criticizing,” says my inner critic.</p>

<p>“Aside from that,” I say. “Have you ever said anything nice about anything that I’ve done?”</p>

<p>“That’s not my job,” says my inner critic. “My job is to criticize. Your job is to do things that I can criticize.</p>

<p>“And I must say, I do my job far better than you do.”</p>
