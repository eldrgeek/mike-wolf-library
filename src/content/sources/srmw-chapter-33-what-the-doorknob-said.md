<p>“Is there no limit to how stupid you can get?” Asked my inner critic. “Now you’re going to get answers from a doorknob?”</p>

<p>“I’m writing this for a purpose,” I said to my inner critic. “I put God in my metanovel for a purpose. God told me to ask the doorknob for a purpose. So I’m going to ask the doorknob.”</p>

<p>“Which doorknob” Asked my inner critic. “There are billions of doorknobs.”</p>

<p>But there weren’t billions of doorknobs. Up until now there had been no doorknobs. There hadn’t even been a reference to a door, much less a doorknob, until the last chapter, which forward-referenced a doorknob, and a very vague reference to a piece of household hardware in Chapter 7, on page 39, as I decided <em>Who’s In The Metanovel?</em> But a forward reference to a doorknob is not a doorknob. And neither is a vague reference to household hardware.</p>

<p>Fortunately this was a self-referential metanovel. The doorknob he needed to talk to would appear when imagined; and he would imagine it when he needed to imagine it; and that would be the next sentence.</p>

<p>I imagined a doorknob. “Are you the doorknob God told me to talk to?” I asked the imaginary doorknob.</p>

<p>“Yes,” said the doorknob.</p>

<p>To save time I imagined that the doorknob had read the previous chapter and knew why it was here.</p>

<p>“How can I remember to practice remembering to practice?” I asked the doorknob.</p>

<p>“The answer is obvious,” said the doorknob.</p>

<p>“It’s not obvious to me,” I said.</p>

<p>“Obviously,” said the doorknob.</p>

<p>“So what’s the answer?” I asked.</p>

<p>“The answer is still obvious,” said the doorknob.</p>

<p><em>Great!</em> I thought. <em>I’ve imagined a doorknob that talks in riddles. I need someone who can actually help.</em></p>

<p>“Obviously,” said the doorknob.</p>

<p>“Imagine someone helpful,” said the metanovel.</p>

<p>“How?” I asked.</p>

<p>“Start a new chapter,” said the metanovel. “Sit down and look at it. Have faith in yourself. Practice! You’ll write the answer.”</p>
