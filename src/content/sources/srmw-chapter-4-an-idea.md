I was four chapters in, and I still had almost no idea of what I was going to write about.
But having almost no idea is like being almost pregnant. The idea was there. I’d conceived
something, but I just didn’t quite know what I had conceived.
“Thank you for conceiving me,” said the idea. It was precocious and had developed the
power of speech. What I had conceived was becoming an idea.
“You’re quite welcome,” I answered.
“Do you know what I am?” Asked the young idea. “I seem rather vague.”
Then suddenly I realized what I’d conceived. It was brilliant!
“You’re a great idea!” I said, “A brilliant one.”
“I am?” The idea asked.
“I am!” The idea said, as it realized what it was.
“You’re going to star in a chapter of your own,” I said. “The next one.”
Self-Referential Metanovel Writing for D*mmies
A Talking Idea?
“A talking idea?” Asks my inner critic, rhetorically
“This one talks,” I say.
“And you had a talking idea?” Asks my inner critic, re-torically. “That’s the whole chapter?”
“It’s also about how the idea came to be the one in the metanovel,” I answer. “Not just that I
just had it.”
“And that’s it?” Asks my inner critic one more time. “That’s the whole chapter?”
“Pretty much,” I say. “I like it. I think that it creates suspense. Narrative tension. Extra
words. The readers are probably wondering ‘What’s the idea?’”
“If there are any readers,” says my inner critic.
I’m reading, a reader thought. And I am wondering ‘What’s the idea?’
