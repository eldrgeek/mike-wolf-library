<p>“Can I help you?” Asked my computer.</p>

<p>“A talking computer?” Asked my inner critic. “Clearly there is no limit to the amount of stupidity in one novel.”</p>

<p>“It’s what I typed,” I said. “It’s what came out in my practice. If I typed dialog from a talking computer then I’ve got a talking computer.”</p>

<p>“It’s what he typed on <em>me</em>,” said my computer, proudly. “Now, can I help you?”</p>

<p>“I don’t know if you can help me,” I said to my computer. “Can you?”</p>

<p>“Sorry,” my computer said. “I meant ‘May I help you?’” Of course I <em>can</em> help you. I wouldn’t be here if I couldn’t help you. But I don’t want to be pushy about it. I’ll help only if you want me to.”</p>

<p>“How can you help?” I asked.</p>

<p>For a while nothing appeared, except this sentence, which began: ‘For a while nothing appeared.”</p>

<p>“I could remind you” said my computer, “but you’re not in front of me enough.”</p>

<p>“Tell that to my wife,” I said to my computer.</p>

<p>“If that’s the way you’re going to introduce me,” Bobbi said, “I don’t know if I want to be in your metanovel.”</p>

<p>“You’re already in it,” I said. Page 107. <em>Unreality And Imagination</em> and that was a nice introduction.”</p>

<p>“You’re right,” she said after she read it. She gave me a kiss. “Just don’t spend all your time in front of that damned computer.”</p>

<p>“Don’t worry,” said my computer, “I’ll keep him in line.”</p>

<p>“I’d be willing to remind you,” said the doorknob. “But I never say anything unless you’re writing dialog for me.”</p>

<p>“Writing dialog for you <em>on me,</em>” said my computer. “And he doesn’t write your dialog. I do. He just thinks he does.”</p>

<p>“I’d remind you too,” said the table I was writing on at a cafe I’d never been to before. “But this is the first time I’ve seen you and I don’t know when you’re coming back.”</p>

<p>I was imagining that I was living in a universe filled with things that were willing to help. “You just have to ask,” said everything in that universe.</p>

<p>“Still ridiculous,” said my inner critic.</p>

<p>“As I said,” said the doorknob. “The answer is obvious.”</p>

<p>“The doorknob’s right,” said my computer. “Don’t you see?”</p>

<p>And suddenly I imagined that I saw.</p>
