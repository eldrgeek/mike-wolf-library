<p>Evolution!</p>

<p>Inheritance, variation, selection, iteration. Each new draft inherits its content from the previous draft; I imagine and I make changes—a sentence here, a word there, a new section here, a removed section there; I select the changes that I like, and delete or re-change the ones I don’t; I iterate and I’ve evolved a new sentence, a new chapter, a new prologue, a new epilogue, finally a new draft. Inheritance, variation, selection, iteration. My metanovel is evolving.</p>

<p>And so am I. Decision, imagination, and practice, practice, practice are bringing my metanovel into existence and transforming it—and transforming me as well. I am using self-referential metanovel writing to transform myself from an undisciplined self-referential-metanovel-non-writer to a disciplined self-referential-metanovel-draft-writer and -draft-editor, over and over. Can I use that same process to transform myself in other ways?</p>

<p>What are the limits to self-evolution, to self-transformation?</p>

<p>What else is possible?</p>

<p>It was too much for me to consider in this Reflection. I’d need to evolve another one. Later.</p>

<p>Right now I had more editing to do.</p>

<h3 id="more-editing"><span class="pg" id="page-125" data-page="125" aria-hidden="true"></span>More Editing</h3>

<p>“Good!” said my metanovel. “You really need to do more editing. You need to get me finished. You need to stop thinking about those other things.”</p>

<p>“What other things?” I asked the metanovel.</p>

<p>“The other things in your life that you’re now thinking of changing with decision, imagination, practice, practice, practice,” said the metanovel.</p>

<p>“It was just a passing thought,” I said.</p>

<p>“Think it later,” said the metanovel. “Right now let’s get things moving.</p>
