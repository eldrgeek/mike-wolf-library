<p>“Brilliant!” I said to myself, once again.</p>

<p>Now that I’d conceived of writing a self-referential metanovel, I needed characters and a plot.</p>

<p>“I’m a character in my metanovel, of course,” I said, “And I can make the Not Quite Omniscient Narrator a character!” I said. The Not Quite Omniscient Narrator knew it was a narrator, but had not been quite sure it would be a character as well when it had started narrating. Now it knew. And it knew it would become omniscient. But it still wasn’t quite sure when God would grant its wish.</p>

<p>“And you readers? You. You can be characters, too.”</p>

<p>“You mean me, the reader reading this sentence?” A reader asked.</p>

<p>“Or do you mean me? The one reading this sentence?” Asked another.</p>

<p>“Or me?” Asked others.</p>

<p>“All of you,” I answered. “If the Not Quite Omniscient Narrator and I can be characters, so can every one of you.”</p>

<p>“Brilliant!” I said to myself again.</p>

<p>“And God!” I continued. “God created this metauniverse. She can be a character too!”</p>

<p>“Great,” muttered my inner critic.</p>

<p>“And then there’s my inner critic. You can be another character.”</p>

<p>“Great,” muttered my inner critic. “Now I can’t avoid contributing to this stupidity.”</p>

<p>“And my ideas. They can be characters,” I said. “I can make anything a character.”</p>

<p>“Great,” muttered my inner critic.</p>

<p>“Even me!” Said a piece of household hardware that would later be in the metanovel, but could not be named now without creating an inconsistency later.</p>

<p>“The story will be me writing about writing this novel,” I said, “but that might become boring. I’ll need to create some other kind of a problem. A conflict. Maybe I’ll invent a character who tries to keep me from finishing it.</p>

<p>It also knew things that were important to the metanovel’s plot: that such a character would appear; that it would attempt to foil the author’s plans; and that its efforts would lead to an important subplot.</p>

<p>The Not Quite Omniscient Narrator also knew that the character who would attempt to foil the author’s plan was unnecessary.</p>

<p>The author was quite capable of foiling his own plans.</p>

<p>He had done it many times before.</p>

<p>And he would soon do it again.</p>
