<p>Practice was getting things moving. I got back to the story. The muse had gotten me upset. It was time to do something about it.</p>

<p>“I don’t have to be upset,” I said to myself. “I don’t have to be depressed. Most of what she said was not real. I can decide to be calm. I can imagine myself calm. I can practice being calm. And most important, I can decide to and imagine myself solving this problem.”</p>

<p>“Can you review the problem?” Asked the metanovel. “Some readers may have forgotten.”</p>

<p>“A problem?” Asked my inner critic. “That would imply some purpose or intention somewhere in this book.”</p>

<p>“I remember what the problem was,” I interrupted and lied. I quickly scrolled back through my draft and reread my interactions with the muse.</p>

<p>“Really. I remember what the problem was,” I said truthfully. “It’s the one where the muse tells me that I have to throw away what I’ve written. It’s in the chapter called “”It’s All Crap!.”</p>

<p>“That was back on page 87,” says my inner critic. “Who can remember that far back?”</p>

<p>“No one needs to,” said the metanovel. “This discussion provides the necessary context. Nice literary device!”</p>

<p>“Than you,” I said. “Now I’m getting back to work.”</p>

<p class="inset center">* * * * *</p>

<p>“Alright,” I said to myself. “I don’t have to be upset. I don’t have to be depressed. I can imagine myself calm. And most important, I can decide to and imagine myself solving this problem.”</p>

<p>“You already said that” Echoed my inner critic.</p>

<p>“Shut up,” said the metanovel to my inner critic. “He’s on a roll.”</p>

<p>“Go ahead,” said the metanovel to me. “Tell us how you imagine solving it.”</p>

<p class="inset center">* * * * *</p>

<p>“I don’t have to be upset,” I said to myself for the third and last time. “I don’t have to be depressed. I can imagine myself calm. And most important, I can imagine myself solving that problem. I have to imagine I can solve it because if I can’t imagine solving it then there’s no chance I can solve it. But if I can imagine solving it, then there is a chance that I can. So I’m going to imagine solving it. I’m going to imagine success.”</p>

<p>To imagine success I believed I’d have to imagine that I was smarter than I currently imagined that I was. If I was smart enough, it would be easy to imagine how to solve it. Then I’d imagine doing whatever it was that I had imagined, and imagine practicing it until I could imagine practicing it really well, and then I would actually do it, and that would take care of it.</p>

<p>I started by imagining I was smarter. Then I reviewed the facts. I’d created the muse to help me, and I didn’t want to pass up her help; but to have her help I’d have to destroy the metanovel. This was a metanovel that I’d conceived, developed, and was bringing to maturity. It had become a friend. I didn’t want to destroy my friend.</p>

<p>“Thank you,” said the metanovel.</p>

<p><span class="pg" id="page-127" data-page="127" aria-hidden="true"></span>Either/Or. Could a more intelligent me find a way that could have both my muse and my metanovel?</p>

<p>Then I imagined the answer.</p>

<p>“I could lie!” I said. “I could lie! I could tell the muse that I’ve destroyed all the copies. Then she’ll help me. But I won’t destroy them all. I’ll still have what I created. And my friend.</p>

<p><em>But that would mean I’d have to lie to her face.</em> I thought. <em>I can’t imagine doing something that dishonest.</em></p>

<p>If I couldn’t imagine it, I couldn’t do it. And if I couldn’t do it my plan would fail. I had only one choice. I changed my mind. I imagined I could lie. I felt pretty good about imagining lying.</p>

<p>I practiced imagining lying. The more I practiced, the realer it got and the realer it got the more I felt that something was wrong.</p>

<p>I imagined meeting the muse. I imagined telling her I’d destroyed everything. “I’ve destroyed everything,” I imagined saying. “You’re lying,” I imagined her replying. “It’s written all over your face.”</p>

<p>I saw it. I did not believe I was a good liar and I believed that the muse was very perceptive. I believed that the power to see through lies that I imagined she had was much greater than the power to lie convincingly that I imagined I had. In my imagined universe, I had already imagined circumstances that made it certain that I would fail. I had imagined failure and that was what I was going to produce.</p>

<p>“But I don’t have to fail,” I said. “I can become a skilled liar. It would just take practice. The more I practice lying, the better liar I’ll be. I imagine that I can become a good enough liar to fool her.”</p>

<p><em>I believe that will work,</em> I thought. “I believe that will work,” I said.</p>

<p>I’d need to take time for lying practice, but that was no problem. I could take an interchapter break and practice for as long as I needed to—for years, if necessary.</p>
