The memory of a song that I’d heard arose in my mind a few days ago. Or maybe it was a few weeks ago. WTF. It arose, OK?

I didn’t remember the name of the song. I vaguely remembered the subject. I did remember that it was part of a musical performance that Speakeasy Theatre in Boston had done when Dana was Production Stage Manager.

I remembered a song about Steve Buschemi and a Thanksgiving Day celebration. I remembered that it had something to do with 9/11.

Never fear. My [Google-fu](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Google-fu) is strong, and shortly I’d found the name of the production: [“Elegies, a song cycle”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elegies_(William_Finn)) by William Finn, and of course, someone had [uploaded the soundtrack to YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tagy-pVqu00).

The song is [Anytime (I Am There)](https://genius.com/William-finn-anytime-i-am-there-lyrics), and the link is to the [Genius.com](https://genius.com/) lyrics site.

The song is from the perspective of someone who died when one of the towers collapsed on 9/11. Even though they are gone, they’ll still be there.

I love the song, and I want to share it with you (and Future Me, so it doesn’t get lost again.)

I plan to organize my memorial service. Knowing me, I’ll procrastinate too long. So if I don’t get around to it, and you are involved in setting it up: please note: I want this played.

I also want Amazing Grace by Dropkick Murphys.

Below are links Any Time on YouTube, the lyrics to Any Time, and following that, a live performance and the album track for Amazing Grace.

These are my dying wishes because all my wishes are dying wishes because I’m dying—though very slowly, and not completing the process for many years.
