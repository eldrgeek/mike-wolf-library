<p>I woke up a 8:00 AM. “Practice, practice, practice,” I said to myself.</p>

<p>It was 9:48. I’d had breakfast, made my list of things to do, talked to Bobbi and one of my kids. “Practice, practice, practice,” I said to myself. And I started this chapter</p>

<p><em>It’s not just a matter of writing the word practice,</em> I thought, <em>although that’s a kind of practice. It’s deliberate practice in any form. It’s practicing imagining, and practicing doing, and meta practicing.</em></p>

<p>“Are you going to write the stupid chapters about practicing until you die?” Asked my inner critic.</p>

<p>“No,” I said. “I’m going to write until I practice consistently.”</p>

<p>“It’s going to get <em>awfully</em> b-o-o-o-o-o-ring,” said my inner critic.</p>

<p>“I’m not bored writing,” I said. “I can always edit it later.”</p>

<p>And I did. I wrote a lot of things that didn’t work. And I deleted them.</p>

<p>“And what’s left is what did work?” Asked my inner critic. “This works?”</p>

<p>“It’s not great,” I said. “Not every page has to be great. But there’s a point to it,”</p>

<p>There was a point to it, the Not Quite Omniscient Narrator narrated, and it was an important point.</p>

<p>By leaving a record of his many failures the author was beginning the final phase of the metanovel.</p>
