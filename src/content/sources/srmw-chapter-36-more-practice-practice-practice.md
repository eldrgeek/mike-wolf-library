I woke up a 8:00 AM. “Practice, practice, practice,” I said to myself.
It was 9:48. I’d had breakfast, made my list of things to do, talked to Bobbi and one of my
kids. “Practice, practice, practice,” I said to myself. And I started this chapter
It’s not just a matter of writing the word practice, I thought, although that’s a kind of prac­
tice. It’s deliberate practice in any form. It’s practicing imagining, and practicing doing, and
meta practicing.
“Are you going to write the stupid chapters about practicing until you die?” Asked my inner
critic.
“No,” I said. “I’m going to write until I practice consistently.”
“It’s going to get awfully b-o-o-o-o-o-ring,” said my inner critic.
“I’m not bored writing,” I said. “I can always edit it later.”
And I did. I wrote a lot of things that didn’t work. And I deleted them.
“And what’s left is what did work?” Asked my inner critic. “This works?”
“It’s not great,” I said. “Not every page has to be great. But there’s a point to it,”
There was a point to it, the Not Quite Omniscient Narrator narrated, and it was an important
point.
By leaving a record of his many failures the author was beginning the final phase of the
metanovel.
