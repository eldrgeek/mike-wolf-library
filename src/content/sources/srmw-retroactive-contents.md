<h3 id="epilogue-2-it-s-really-done"><span class="pg" id="page-25" data-page="25" aria-hidden="true"></span>Epilogue 2: It’s Really Done!</h3>

<p><em>It worked!</em> I think to myself. <em>Look at those Retroactive Contents!</em></p>

<p>I remember how I had written my first draft. I had put down whatever came to mind, trying to get my word count up—and trying to encourage myself. En-courage myself. Giving myself the courage to start, and the courage to keep going and most of all, the courage to finish. <em>If I keep imagining that I’m succeeding before I fail, and if I keep imagining that I’m finished before I start, then maybe I’ll actually succeed and finish,</em> I think I thought.</p>

<p>“Stupid then, stupid now” says my inner critic.</p>

<p>“Ignore the critic,” say God and the metanovel together.</p>

<p><em>Whatever it was, it worked!</em> I think. <em>I didn’t care what was in the book. I wasn’t trying to produce a great book. Not even a good book. Not even a lousy book. A total, utter, complete piece-of-shit book would have been fine as long as it was a complete, 50,000 word book.</em></p>

<p>“You seem be having trouble producing even a 50,000 word piece of shit,” says my inner critic, later in my first draft. I was struggling, trying to get the word count up to 50,000 before time ran out.</p>

<p>“I did have trouble, but I got it done,” I say still later, after I’d written 51,321 words. “And it was 51,321 words when it was done.”</p>

<p>“A 51,321 word piece of shit is still a piece of shit,” says my inner critic. “And it’s not done.”</p>

<p>“What do you mean it’s not done?” I ask.</p>

<p>“You haven’t finished editing,” says my inner critic.</p>

<p>“It’s getting better,” I say much later, as I struggle with my edits.</p>

<p>“Improved shit is still shit,” my inner critic says, over and over. And over. And over.</p>

<p><em>Now it’s nearly time to stop writing this first draft of the epilogue</em>, I remembered that I had thought, as I had nearly finished writing the first draft of this epilogue. <em>And next it will be time to write the first word in the first sentence in the first draft of the first chapter.</em></p>

<p>“It’s going to be a stupid chapter,” my inner critic complained. “Just like this epilogue. I can tell.”</p>
