So I'm back writing. And I have two choices: I can clear the backlog, the drafts that I started, some already pre-dated, or I can write new stuff--stuff about what I'm interested in right now, and about things I'm going to work on in the near future--rather than what I thought was interesting two weeks ago.

Put that way, the decision is easy. Write what's interesting now. Look forward. Then, I believe, the writing's going to flow.

And it's flowing. My first draft of [my first post today](http://70yearsoldwtf.blogspot.com/2013/01/i-give-up-now-i-can-start.html) was just few paragraphs long. Each time I edited it ideas flowed into it. It became more of a story, more interesting to me, and I hope more interesting to others.

But the truth is: I don't care if you like it. My writing is for me.

So, you might ask, if you're still here: "How does one 'write new stuff Gangnam style?'"

Easy, I answer. You go to YouTube. You stick earphones in your ears. You go to [this page](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9bZkp7q19f0), the official version of PSY's Gangnam Style video. And you play it. Over and over as you write. Every once in a while you look up and wish your knees weren't so fucked up and you could dance that way. It looks like fun.

But those days are gone, probably forever.

Then you remember the great mashup of Gangnam Style by a team at [MIT](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), your alma mata. So you Google it, find it and play it. The video is [here on YouTube](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9bZkp7q19f0), and allegedly you can play on your mobile device if you go to [this link](http://mobileyt.com/21678457.php). And to make it easy, embedded below.

MIT's version starts with PSY's performance with MIT students lip syncing, and doing the posturing and the dancing. The second part has a cover by the MIT singing group the [Logarhythms](http://maps.google.com/maps?ll=42.360007,-71.08787&spn=0.01,0.01&q=42.360007,-71.08787%20(Traditions%20and%20student%20activities%20at%20MIT)&t=h) with more MIT video including a guest appearance by [Noam Chomsky](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky).
