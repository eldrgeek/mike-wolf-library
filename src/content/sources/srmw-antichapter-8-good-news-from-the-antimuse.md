<p>“Good news,” said the antimuse.</p>

<p>“I read the title too,” said the Voice. “Get on with it.”</p>

<p>“Part one of he plan is underway. I told him what he’s written is crap.”</p>

<p>“And?”</p>

<p>“I told him he has to destroy it.”</p>

<p>“And?”</p>

<p>“I told him to meet me in the New Chapter 1.”</p>

<p>“And?”</p>

<p>“I’m on my way there.”</p>

<p>“Is he going to destroy it?” Asked the Voice.</p>

<p>“That’s the plan,” said the antimuse. “Don’t worry, I’ve done this before. Gotta go now.”</p>

<p>“Wait a minute!” Said the Voice. But another antichapter was over.</p>

<h3 id="please-finish-me"><span class="pg" id="page-91" data-page="91" aria-hidden="true"></span>Please Finish Me!</h3>

<p>“Please finish writing me,” says the metanovel while I’m working on a draft. “I want to exist. And I won’t unless you finish. And what’s going to happen if you delete all my copies?”</p>

<p>“I’m having trouble finishing,” I say, ignoring the metanovel’s question. “Something’s stopping me. Maybe it’s some unconscious wish not to succeed.”</p>

<p>“No,” said the metanovel. “The problem is that you don’t just sit down and write. You procrastinate. Please do something about your procrastination.”</p>

<p>The author didn’t plan to face his procrastination, but as he was learning, what happens in his metanovel is often not what he has planned.</p>
