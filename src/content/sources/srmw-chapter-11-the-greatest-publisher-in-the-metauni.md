“What do you think,” I asked, anxiously. “Is it publishable.”
I sat nervously in the office I’d created of the character I’d created to publish the book I was
creating.
I studied him as I waited. He was tall, and handsome, and moved with astonishing grace. He
was brilliant and articulate. In his presence I found myself both diminished and elevated; dimin­
ished because I realized I’d never be half the man he was; elevated because something in his
manner made me feel special, as though, for that moment at least, I was the most important per­
son in the universe. Even though he was.
“I’ve read what you’ve written so far,” he said.
“Including this chapter?” I asked.
“Right to the sentence before this one,” he said. “It’s not publishable.”
“Why not?” I asked.
“Not enough sex,” he said. “Sex sells books, You’ve got 46 pages in this draft, including this
page, and not one bit of sex. You need sex. A lot of sex. And you have to fix the opening part of
this chapter where you describe me.”
“It’s an accurate description, isn’t it?” I asked. “Aren’t you short, fat, and grotesquely ugly.
Isn’t your face covered with warts and pustular sores?”
“Four four isn’t that short,” objected The Greatest Publisher In The Metauniverse. “Fix it if
you want me to publish it.”
“Maybe I’ll go to another publisher,” I said. “I’m not about to be blackmailed. I have artistic
integrity.”
“There are no other publishers in this metauniverse,” The Greatest Publisher In The
Metauniverse replied, smiling. “You are not about to be blackmailed; you had already been
blackmailed when you said you were not about be blackmailed. And you are still being black­
mailed. And you are about to learn how little artistic integrity you have.”
The Greatest Publisher In The Metauniverse was telling the truth, the Not Quite Omniscient
Narrator narrated, regretfully. There were no other publishers. The author had been and was be­
ing blackmailed. And he was about to find out that he had no artistic integrity.
“OK,” I said, “I’ll add more sex. How do you want me to describe you?”
“Here,” he said, and handed me a piece of paper. “Use this.”
I looked at what he’d written. It was a complete fabrication. But I decided to use it. Using it
would show that I wasn’t rigid, but flexible and open minded, good attributes in a writer.
It doesn’t show that at all,” said my inner critic. “It shows that you have no artistic
integrity.”
“Your inner critic is right, said The Publisher. “And one other thing,” the Publisher added.
“What is it?” I asked.

He’s Right, You Know.
“Who?” I asked my inner critic, after reading the title.
“The Greatest Publisher In The Metauniverse,” said my inner critic. “He’s the voice of your
unconscious. Your unconscious knows. Even if you won’t admit it.”
“That I need more sex?” I asked.
“No,” said my inner critic. “That your book sucks.”
I thought for a moment. Actually I thought for several weeks, but to you it will seem like a

“I’m back to my original goal,” I said. “I don’t care how good it is. I don’t care about pub­

happens.”
“Alright,” said my inner critic. “But I want to point out, for your own good, that your book

Enough Sex For Publication
In 2011 in this metanovel’s parent universe, the population of planet Earth had reached sev­
en billion people. Since metauniverses inherit all properties of their parent universes unless spe­
cifically overridden, that made the population of metaEarth in the metauniverse seven billion
people as well.
Human sex acts, including masturbation, by sexually active human individuals, occur, on av­
erage, 3.714 times per week. The average is raised substantially by teenaged boys. In both uni­
verses, half of those seven billion people are too young, too old, too sick, or prevented by occu­
pation, belief, or circumstances from engaging in sex. That leaves left 3.5 billion sexually active
individuals who would among them engage in almost 13 billion human sex acts per week. The
average sex act takes 4.122 minutes and requires 1.2 people (most sex acts are masturbation and
most masturbation is fast and solo.) As a result, human beings spend 153 million person-hours
per day having sex.
That’s a lot of sex.
To put it in perspective: it took just seven million person-hours to build the Empire State
building, and 22 million person-hours to build the Golden Gate Bridge. Humans engage in
nearly seven Golden Gates bridges or 22 Empire State buildings worth of sex each and every
day.
