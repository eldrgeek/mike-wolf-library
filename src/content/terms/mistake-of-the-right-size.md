<p><strong>What we mean.</strong> A calibration rule for risk-taking. Too small a mistake teaches nothing; too big a one does real damage. The right size is a bounded-cost, reversible experiment — big enough to learn from, small enough that being wrong is cheap. It's the license to <em>act</em> rather than deliberate: pick something reversible and try it. Much of the estate's "default to proceeding" habit rests on this.</p>
