Bobbi and I are watching a [Great Courses](http://www.thegreatcourses.com/) series titled "The Science of Mindfulness: A Research-based Path to Well-Being" taught by Professor Ronald Siegel of Harvard. The course itself has been worthwhile, and I've dug into several related practices that the course mentions, one where I quit after about a quarter-inch of digging, the other in which I've dug more deeply.

The quarter-inch depth digging was into another course, called "Mindfulness Based Stress Reduction" (MBSR) (Wikipedia ref, [here](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindfulness-based_stress_reduction).)

While I am sure that none of you have problems with stress, you probably have friends who do. They would probably appreciate your doing some follow-up research, and passing information to them.

You can also find out more about MBSR following some of the links I've collected below.

It was developed originally by [Jon Kabat-Zinn](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Kabat-Zinn) at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center. I've never heard of Siegel before, but I have heard of Kabat-Zinn, who is a medium-sized deal in some parts of the tech community--medium-sized enough that Google invited him to do a one-hour [Tech Talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rSU8ftmmhmw).

Other links I found because that's what I do:

A free MBSR online self-paced course, [here](http://palousemindfulness.com/selfguidedMBSR.html) modelled after Kabat-Zinn's. I have no idea if it is any good. I just googled and it appeared.

There are some other resources at the [UMass Mindfulness center](http://www.umassmed.edu/cfm/)

A bunch of [videos](http://www.umassmed.edu/cfm/resources/videos/) on the mindfulness center's resource page.
