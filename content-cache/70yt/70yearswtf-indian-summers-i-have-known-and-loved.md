I’m stumped.

Here I am, at the Deer Isle (Maine) Writers’ group, with a bunch of other 70- and 80-year-old and maybe even 90-year-old retired white people. The writing prompt is “Indian summers I have known and loved.”

Really?

Indian Summers?

Who’s been to India?

Judith, a good friend and host to today’s gathering, gave us the prompt. I’m pretty sure she knows that Bobbi and I have never been within several thousand miles of India. Time for a quick fact-check: Japan is three thousand seven hundred for me; more for Bobbi. Thank you, Google.

I don’t know Judith’s whole travel history, but I know a lot, and I’m pretty sure she hasn’t been to India either.

So why that prompt?

Why Indian summers and not Maine summers?

## Maine summers

Summers in Maine are glorious. There’s a saying, (best heard from someone with an authentic Mainer accent): “If ya cahn’t stahnd the wintahs, ya don’t dhesehve the summahs.” It’s true.

We’re year-rounders, and we earn our Maine summah's. But Indian summers. (With Indian accent): “**ɪndɪən ˈsʌmərz**” What’s the idea?

Judith, what were you thinking?

That’s when I realize that I’m missing something.

## Engineering and poetry

I’m an engineer. Judith is a poet. So maybe interpreting “Indian Summers” literally, like an engineer, is wrong. Maybe I need to interpret it metaphorically, like a poet.

Poets use metaphors. Maybe I need some metaphors.

Great.

Where am I going to find metaphorical meanings for Indian Summers?

I know the answer as soon as I ask the question. It’s obvious. It would be obvious to any engineer. Ask [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com/). Or [Bard](https://bard.google.com/chat). Or [Claude.ai.](https://claude.ai/chats) Or [LLaMA](https://ai.meta.com/llama/). Or your favorite [Large Language Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) (LLM).

Luckily, like nearly all the other 70- and 80-year-old and maybe even 90-year-old retired white people who have hosted Writers’ Group, Judith has high-speed internet. I’ve got the password. I’ve got my computer. I’ve got everything I need to discover the metaphorical meaning

I open a tab for each LLM and ask each. Every one of them comes through.

## The world of meaning

There’s a  world of meaning out there, and Google and my LLMs are always ready to help me find meaning.

I checked each one, and Claude.ai has the best answers.

Or at least Claude the most answers. I’m an engineer, not a poet. How would I know what’s best? But I can count what’s m,ost!

But here are Claude’s nine answers. An Indian Summer is:

- A fleeting period of joy, freedom, or contentment that ends too soon.
- A time of exploration and new experiences that broadens one's horizons.
- A bittersweet ending to a lovely experience that must come to a close.
- A calm before the storm; a reprieve of easy times before more difficult ones.
- A time of creativity, passion, and being in tune with one's spirit.
- A period of growth, change, and transformation from one state to another.
- A time of appreciation for the simple joys in life.
- A reminder that seasons and stages inevitably come and go.
- A gift or blessing that arrives unexpectedly.

Alright, Claude! Of course! We’ve got this!

## A new meaning

Judith, how could I ever have doubted you?

You meant so much I didn’t see by “Indian Summers.”

Even as an engineer, I could write thousands of words about each of those metaphorical meanings.

If I were a poet, I could write more.

Or a lot less.

## My Indian Summers

According to those metaphorical meanings, my life has been a years-long year-round unfolding procession of Indian Summers.

Every summer, an Indian Summer.

Sounds weird, to say, but every winter, an Indian Summer, too.

Maybe that’s poetic.

I don’t know.

I’m just an engineer.

But it seems cool,

So now I know what to write about. But where shall I start?

## Where do I start?

This a silly question. As soon as I ask it, the answer is obvious.

I’ve already started.

I started with, “I’m stumped.”

I’ve been going for nearly 600 words.

So maybe the question is: where shall I end?

Another silly question. Once again, the answer is obvious.

Right here.
