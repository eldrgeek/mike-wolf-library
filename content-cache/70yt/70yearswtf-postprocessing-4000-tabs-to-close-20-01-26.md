For several years I’ve been using a tool called [OneTab](https://www.one-tab.com/). It’s a [Chrome plugin](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/onetab/chphlpgkkbolifaimnlloiipkdnihall) that closes tabs and adds them to a list of tabs that it maintains on each computer.

I’m a victim of TPD—Tab Proliferation Disorder. When I discover I’ve got too many tabs open at once—and by “too many” I often mean 30 or more—I lose them with OneTab. That way, I get my (the computer’s) memory back without losing my memory of what tabs I had open.

My workhorse computer is now up to nearly 4000 tabs, and I’ve got a bunch of closed tabs on other machines. So today, I decided to start closing more than I open.

That decision led to an intention and a plan as I’ve [written about here](https://70yearsoldwtf.blogspot.com/2020/01/attention-on-intention-and-metaintention.html).

A lot of the tabs are repeats. Many of them are programs I have pen regularly, like Gmail, or StackEdit, or even OneTab.

So I plan to go through the tabs, deleting the ones that I can, opening the ones I’d like to not forget about. I’ll write some of them in [this blog](https://70yearsoldwtf.blogspot.com/) and some in another, [Random S*&# I Learned Today](https://rsilt.blogspot.com/).

I’ll tag both the posts that come from OneTab in both blogs with “OneTab.”

I’ll keep track of how many posts I have left to close here.
